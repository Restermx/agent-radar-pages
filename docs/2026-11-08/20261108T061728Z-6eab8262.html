Enhancing Local and Global Structure in Compressed AI Agent Memory ↗

arXiv · Agent · 2026-11-05 论文摘要 预印本

作者研究 Agent 记忆压缩中的局部与全局结构保真，指出保留近邻不一定保留较远记忆间的关系，并提出 LAQ 量化目标。摘要报告其在多个等容量实验中改善全局保真；结果及理论需结合全文核对。

为什么值得读　适合关注长期记忆、向量压缩和检索评测的人，提醒评测不应只看最近邻保持率。

Henry Han
阅读原论文摘要 · Original abstract

AI agents store text as embedding vectors and compress them at scale. A compression is accepted when each item's nearest neighbors survive, but agents also consolidate, delete, and compare memories, tasks that depend on which items lie far apart. We evaluate both ends with two label-free fidelities from our prior work: the local fidelity $L$ at 10 neighbors and the global fidelity $G$ at 30 to 50 percent of the memory. At 96 bytes per passage, rescored binary codes reach an $L$ of 0.97 yet move 10.1 percent of passages to another theme; PCA with 8-bit codes, at an $L$ of 0.77, moves 1.7 percent. Rescoring leaves the stored codes, and thus $G$, unchanged. We prove that across the $G$ band, $1-ν_k$ is at most 2 to 2.18 times the square root of the near-far rank-violation rate, and that this rate follows the distance noise of the compression (correlation 0.979). Our locality-aware quantization (LAQ) turns the bound into a hinge objective on the codebooks of an OPQ index, keeping its bytes, rotation, and search. To our knowledge, LAQ is the first quantizer whose objective comes from a proven bound on the far end. Reconstruction alone raises $G$ in 0 of 52 held-out runs; the far hinge raises it in all 52. LAQ raises $G$ in all 52 ($p=8.5\times 10^{-4}$ over 13 memory-encoder groups), including the external FinDER memory, has the highest $G$ of seven equal-size quantizers in all 12 Multi-Doc-2025 runs, scales to a million passages, and keeps evidence recall.

摘要依据
AI agents store text as embedding vectors and compress them at scale.

Hard Issue, Weak Model, or Bad Run? Rethinking Failure Prediction for Coding Agents ↗

arXiv · Agent · 2026-11-05 论文摘要 预印本

论文质疑编码 Agent 的轨迹失败预测是否真正捕捉运行状态。作者报告，在其 SWE-bench 轨迹分析中，任务难度与模型能力解释了主要预测表现，并呼吁使用重复运行、等预算闭环评测区分失败原因。

为什么值得读　适合设计重试、模型切换和早停策略时阅读，避免把预测相关性直接当作有效决策依据。

Xingyue Shi · Wesley K. G. Assunção
阅读原论文摘要 · Original abstract

Trajectory-based failure predictors for software engineering (SE) agents read the partial trajectory of a run to flag whether it is going right or wrong. If it is going wrong, the run can be stopped, retried, or switched to another model. We ask whether these predictors learn anything about the run itself. On public SWE-bench Verified trajectories, where each model attempts each issue once, two run-independent factors dominate predictor performance. The first is issue difficulty: a reference score that never looks at the run, only at how hard the issue is for other models, reaches 0.92 AUROC, whereas trajectory predictors reach about 0.62. The second is model ability: within an issue, knowing which model ran predicts failure better than our predictors do, and the predictors largely reproduce the overall ability ranking of the models. Beyond difficulty and ability, what predictors gain from the trajectory is statistically equivalent to zero. This confusion carries over to the actions taken after a prediction. Model switching is often motivated by the gap between the best single model's resolution rate and an any-model oracle. This gap is no larger than random variation alone produces. Per-issue switching gains nothing on held-out runs. In a repeated-run pilot, the same model often solves an issue in some runs and fails it in others. Retrying thus has real room to repair failures, but it rescues only failures caused by a bad run, not those on an issue beyond the model's ability. Current predictors cannot tell the causes apart, as they mostly predict the issue and the model, not the run. We call for failure prediction that explains why a run fails, evaluated on repeated-run benchmarks and in closed-loop, equal-budget settings.

摘要依据
two run-independent factors dominate predictor performance.

SPILLWAY: Separating Target Admission from Sanitization to Defend Tool-Using Agents against Prompt Injection ↗

arXiv · Agent · 2026-11-04 论文摘要 预印本

SPILLWAY 将工具调用目标的准入判断与文本净化分开，利用工具结构化返回中的来源信息建立确定性检查。摘要报告了多组攻击实验中的防御结果；这些结果限定于作者测试设置，不能据此声称消除所有提示注入。

为什么值得读　与读取网页、邮件和文件后执行工具的 Agent 直接相关，可进一步研究结构化数据和自由文本的职责边界。

Woosang Lim · Yeseul Chang · Jaewoo Lee
阅读原论文摘要 · Original abstract

Tool-using agents act on email, files, and payments, so an instruction hidden in a web page or a shared document can make an agent send data or money to an attacker. Many runtime defenses put a language model on the decision that lets an action through, so the decision depends on a model that may read the attacker's text and adds model calls to every step. Provenance defenses keep the model off the final check but need a model to plan or route each task first. Where an action goes can instead be read from the tools' typed returns: an email's sender or a payment's account arrives in a typed field, while injected instructions arrive as free text. SPILLWAY turns this observation into a deterministic floor with no per-task plan. The check admits a call when each target value that appears in tool output but not in the user's task was returned by a read-only tool in a typed field or is covered by a fixed delegation rule. A deterministic sanitizer removes instruction-like regions its fixed rules match before the model reads them and retains the removed spans as evidence, and a local judge may restore a refused call, never one whose target lies in the removed text. Across three backbones, and seven task suites on one of them, no standard templated injection redirects an action to an attacker's target under SPILLWAY, at the highest observed mean utility and composite scores (NRP, ERS) of the defenses we run under that attack. A forged chat template also produces a raw zero, and mean in-scope attack success under a released reinforcement-learning attacker is .007. SPILLWAY reaches this floor with no defense-triggered backbone re-run and almost no added model call. As personal AI agents act on users' email, files, and payments, SPILLWAY gives them an auditable refusal floor on where their actions go that keeps them useful and holds across backbones.

Take It From Here: Evaluating Coding Agents On Project Takeover ↗

arXiv · Agent · 2026-11-05 论文摘要 预印本

论文提出 ReqMemBench，评测编码 Agent 从历史交互中恢复需求并接手已有项目的能力。作者报告，在其六组模型与执行框架配置、37 个可验证目标中，完整交付率仍较低，困难同时涉及需求状态恢复与代码实现。

为什么值得读　贴近长期项目交接场景，适合评估历史需求、记忆文件和验收测试是否真的帮助 Agent 接续工作。

Dingxian Wang · Zhe Fu · Jiaqi Deng · Chao Yin · Ganesh Prabhu
阅读原论文摘要 · Original abstract

Coding agents increasingly collaborate on long-term projects. When taking over a project, they need to recover currently applicable requirements from the interaction history and update them in response to new requests. However, most coding benchmarks do not include long interaction histories, and some evaluations that consider history still do not cover the full process from requirement reconstruction to project delivery. We therefore provide agents with the interaction history, current request, and pre-request project environment, ask them to make the requested changes, and assess takeover performance through delivery tests. Across six model-harness configurations and 37 targets eligible for executable validation, the mean complete-delivery rate under Full History is only 4.95%. We then use requirement atoms to independently diagnose a larger set of takeover targets, examining relevant information identification, pre-request state reconstruction, and current request processing. We further supply correct pre-request or target states to examine the remaining difficulties in requirement processing and project modification. The diagnostics show that none of the six configurations produces fully correct post-request requirement states, and a case illustrates omission of previously recovered constraints during updating. With correct target states supplied, the mean complete-delivery rate is 8.11%, with build and regression pass rates of 96.40% and 90.99%, but target-test success remains 9.01%. These results show difficulties in both forming target requirement states and implementing supplied targets in existing projects. Based on these findings, we introduce ReqMemBench, which combines requirement diagnostics with delivery validation to support systematic evaluation of project takeover.

摘要依据
When taking over a project, they need to recover currently applicable requirements from the interaction history
